---
title: "Goals"
description: "Set one small goal, plan around what gets in the way, and finish it."
---

A goal in Keel is something small you can finish in days or weeks, like running 5 km or reading one book. Keel asks for the parts that make a goal more likely to get done: what done looks like, a deadline, the thing most likely to get in the way, and a plan for it.

## Set a goal

```text
goal new "Run 5 km"
```

Keel asks one question at a time:

<Steps>
  <Step title="What does done look like?">Something you could check: `I run 5 km in one go`.</Step>
  <Step title="Deadline">`nov 8`, `+3w` or `2026-11-08`.</Step>
  <Step title="How much time will you give it?">`30 minutes a day`. Press Enter to skip.</Step>
  <Step title="What could get in the way?">One thing: `rainy mornings`.</Step>
  <Step title="Your if-then plan">`If it rains at 7:00, then I will do 20 minutes on the treadmill`. It has to read "If ..., then ...".</Step>
  <Step title="The steps">One per question. Make the first one take under 10 minutes. For each step, Keel asks when and where (`sat 7:00 at the park`) and a smaller version for a bad day (`10 minutes`). An empty line ends the list.</Step>
</Steps>

```text
✓ g1 Run 5 km · Sun 8 Nov · 4 steps
  First step: Put my shoes by the door. When it's done: goal step done g1 1
```

You can give any of the answers in one line, and Keel asks only for the rest. Each `step:` adds a step, without a time or place:

```text
goal new "Read a book" done:"last page" due:+3w obstacle:"tired evenings" plan:"If I am tired, then I will read 2 pages" step:"pick the book" step:"read chapter 1"
```

With `--json`, nothing is asked, so give `done:`, `due:`, `obstacle:`, `plan:` and at least one `step:`.

### A deadline that allows for being late

Most plans take longer than we expect. Once you have finished a goal or two, Keel compares how long your last three finished goals took with what you planned. If they ran at least 10% late, it offers a later deadline right after you give yours:

```text
◆ Your last 2 goals took 30% longer than planned. Tue 17 Nov would allow for that.
? Use Tue 17 Nov instead?  y · enter keeps Sun 8 Nov
```

Dropped goals don't count, since they never reached the finish line. A deadline given inline (`due:`) is kept as you wrote it.

## On your phone

With `keel serve` running, open `/goals` on your phone (see [phone login](/guides/phone-login) to reach it from outside your computer). Tap **+ New goal**:

1. **The finish line:** the goal, what done means, the deadline and the time you will give it.
2. **The plan for the hard part:** what is most likely to stop you, then your plan as two blanks, "If ..." and "then I will ...". Keel needs the plan to save. Then add steps, the first one tiny, each with an optional time, place and minimum version.

Once past goals ran late, a note under the deadline says so and offers a later day: tap **Use Thu 12 Nov** to take it (see [a deadline that allows for being late](#a-deadline-that-allows-for-being-late)).

**Start goal** saves it, and Today shows it with its progress and its next step. Tap the round check to mark that step done, or tap the goal to see all of it: what done means, your plan, the steps still open (tick any of them) and the ones already done. Goals made on the phone and in the CLI are the same goals.

To finish, tap **Close goal** on the goal's page. Choose **Done** or **Dropped on purpose**, see how long it took against the plan, and answer the same three questions as `goal done` (each one optional). **Close and save review** closes it.

### When your computer sleeps

The phone app talks to `keel serve` on your computer. When it can't reach it, the app still opens and shows the goals as your phone last saw them, marked **Not live** with the time. Nothing can change until Keel is back: a step you tick is not saved for later, and Keel says so. Tap **Try again** once `keel serve` is running. To keep Goals live all day, run `keel serve` on a machine that stays on.

## See your goals

```text
goals            # active goals
goals --all      # closed ones too
goal show g1     # one goal and its steps (show g1 works too)
```

Progress shows the smaller part: `1 of 4 done` until halfway, then `2 left`.

`today` lists your active goals under **GOALS**, each with its next step and when and where you planned it:

```text
GOALS
  g1  Run 5 km   1 of 4 done   Sun 8 Nov
      next: Run 2 km at the park · today 07:00 · the park
```

The Friday [review](/guides/weekly-review) goes through each goal too: the steps you did this week, then keep going, close it as done, or drop it (with the three review questions below). `summary` counts the steps done on each goal in the week. A `+client` view leaves goals out, since a goal belongs to you, not a client.

## Move it along

```text
goal step done g1 2
```

Keel then shows the next open step. A step can be done once: if you marked it on another device first, Keel says when.

When the goal is finished, or you decide to stop on purpose:

```text
goal done g1
goal drop g1
```

Keel shows how long it took against the plan, then asks three short questions. Each one can be skipped with Enter:

```text
◆ Planned 27 days, took 31: 4 days over.
  Your answers help pad the deadline of your next goal.
? What happened, against the plan?
? Why?
? What will you change next time?
```

`goal show g1` shows the answers under **REVIEW**, and `export --md` includes them. To answer in one line, give any of `happened:`, `why:` and `change:`, and Keel asks none of them:

```text
goal drop g2 happened:"Lost interest after week 1" change:"Pick a goal I want, not one I should"
```

With `--json`, the questions are skipped. Closing a goal from the Friday review asks the same three questions.

A closed goal stays closed. If you closed it on another device first, Keel says so before asking anything. Start a new one with `goal new`.
